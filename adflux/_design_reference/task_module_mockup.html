The shelf has no buttons. On purpose.

No nudge, no chase, no comment. Chasing is the system's job — and the escalation sentence is printed on the card so the hours ticking up read as machinery working, not as a graveyard.

“Ready now” is never allowed to be empty

It backfills from the month's remaining retainer reels. An empty list would tell you he is idle on a day he is carrying eight hours of work — and nobody tolerates a screen that misrepresents them.

He plans in days, not hours

A reel is a multi-day job, so “This week” carries rough durations. No ten-row list — wrong instrument for his kind of work.

Blank on a good day is the product

But it always states a fact it had to look up — “14 jobs running, next deadline Sunday” — otherwise an empty screen is indistinguishable from a broken one.

Every section vanishes when empty

No zeros, no green all-clear banner, no charts. The one chart that exists — hours by work type — lives on its own monthly page, because it is a pricing question, not an alert.

“Delivered outside the app” keeps the list honest

When a designer WhatsApps a file directly, that job would sit here as late forever and you would stop trusting the page. One tap closes it and adds to the footer tally — so you can see how often it happens.

No per-person output. Anywhere.

No ranking, no leaderboard, no output-per-designer. The day the team believes this is a monitoring tool is the day the time tracking gets gamed — and that data is what every screen above runs on.

Due-today does not appear before 3 PM

A due-today list at ten in the morning is noise, and noise is what trains you to stop opening the page.
