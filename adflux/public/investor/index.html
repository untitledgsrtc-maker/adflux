What we are

A digital-out-of-home network on a captive, government-anchored audience.

LED screens inside GSRTC bus stations, in commuters' eyeline while they wait 10–40 minutes with nothing to do. Advertisers are government departments (via DAVP) and private brands. AI cameras report impressions and audience. This is the whole business — one product, one network. No side lines.

Captive

attention we own

Commuters can't scroll past a screen in front of them. Dwell time becomes impressions — the one thing Google and Meta can't sell.

Govt-anchored

demand base

GSRTC moves millions daily. Government advertises heavily on its own network — a large, recurring, DAVP-priced base of demand.

Under-digitised

medium

Most Indian OOH is still static hoardings. Digital LED with live analytics is early — we already run it at scale.

The asset — and the gap

The screens are built. 98% of the ad-time is empty.

Across the current 20 stations we run 264 screens. Each screen can carry 151,200 ten-second ad-slots a month. We sell about 2% of them. The asset exists; the demand is what we're funding. Growth here is not a capacity problem — it's a fill problem.

The unlock

Why 2% — and what takes it to 10%.

We're at 2% not because demand doesn't exist, but because the government rate card (DAVP pricing) for these screens is still being finalised. Departments can't book at scale until the price is set. The moment DAVP prices land, the government demand that already flows through GSRTC can be booked against our screens.

Assumptions & risks — stated plainly

What has to be true.

  • DAVP timing. The fill thesis assumes DAVP pricing lands and government demand follows within ~6 months. If it slips, fill ramps slower — the forecast slider shows that case.
  • Fill is demand-led. The screens exist; the ad-time has to be sold. The plan funds a private-brand sales team precisely because fill is the whole return.
  • Government concentration. Most revenue is government and cyclical with election spend. We diversify into private LED — the higher-margin, non-cyclical line.
  • Taluka-town demand. The 98 are smaller towns. Local-advertiser demand there is less proven than the city hubs — a reason the private-sales investment matters.
  • Capital intensity. At today's 2% fill, LED build takes years to recover. The investment case rests on fill rising — not on the status quo.
  • Unaudited accounts. FY24-25 & FY25-26 audits and the two-entity consolidation are in progress — a condition precedent to funding.
